Seeing Percents

Four short steps. Each one turns a percent into a picture: a square of 100, then a money bar cut into equal boxes. Do them together, in order, with Autumn moving the sliders.

For grown-ups: Why percents are tricky
  • A percent isn't an amount by itself. 25% of $40 is $10, but 25% of $200 is $50. It's always a percent of something, so she needs to see the whole first.
  • The app asks two questions in a row. First the discount (the part that comes off), then the sale price (what's left). Those are different pieces of the same bar.
  • "What percent?" goes backwards. She knows the dollars and has to work out the share. Counting equal boxes turns that into counting.
Step 1

Percent means "out of 100"

Here are 100 squares. Color some in. The number of colored squares is the percent. Each full row is 10 squares, so each row is 10%.

Step 2

Cut the money into 10 equal boxes

Any price can be cut into 10 equal boxes. Each box is 10%. To find one box, move the decimal point one place left. Half a box is 5%.

Step 3

Take it off, or add it on?

A sale cuts a piece off the bar, so you pay less than 100%. Tax and fees stick a piece on the end, so you pay more than 100%. Always ask: which piece does the question want?

Step 4

Find the percent by counting boxes

When the question asks "what percent?", cut the price into 20 small boxes. Each small box is 5%. Count how many boxes the dollars fill, then count by 5s.
