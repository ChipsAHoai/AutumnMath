Seeing Multiplication

Multiplying is counting equal groups fast. Line the groups up in rows and you get a rectangle of dots. Every trick for the times tables is a way of cutting that rectangle into pieces you already know.

For grown-ups: Why times tables are hard
  • Memorizing 144 separate facts feels endless. Turning the rectangle around cuts that almost in half, and the easy tables (×1, ×2, ×5, ×10) cover a lot of the rest.
  • Skip-counting from the start is slow for 7s, 8s and 12s. Splitting into known pieces is faster: 7 × 8 = 5 × 8 + 2 × 8.
  • The app's hint already draws rows of dots and skip-counts. This page adds the steps that come after that.
Step 1

Equal groups make rows

3 × 4 means 3 groups with 4 in each. Put each group in a row and count by the group size down the side.

Step 2

Turn it around

Turn the rectangle on its side. Same dots, so the same answer. If she knows 3 × 8, she also knows 8 × 3.

Step 3

Split it into facts you know

Don't know 7 × 8? Cut the rows into two pieces you do know, then add them. Try cutting at 5 or 2: those are the easy ones.

Step 4

A trick for every table

Pick a times table. Each one is built from the 1s, 2s, 5s or 10s.
