Seeing LCM & GCF

LCM is about things lining up again. GCF is about cutting into the biggest equal pieces. Four steps: see each one as a picture, learn to tell them apart, then the fast way for big numbers.

For grown-ups: Why these are tricky
  • The names sound alike and use the same two numbers. The only difference is whether the answer is bigger or smaller than those numbers.
  • The word problems never say "LCM" or "GCF". She has to tell from the story: bells meeting up again, or ribbons cut into pieces.
  • The app's hint starts with prime factors. That's the fast way, but it only makes sense after she has seen what the answer means.
Step 1 · LCM, least common multiple

When do they line up again?

Two bells ring together at the start. Press Ring to move time forward. Each bell hops ahead by its own number. The first spot where both land is the LCM.

Step 2 · GCF, greatest common factor

What's the biggest piece that fits both?

Cut both ribbons into pieces of the same length. Slide the piece size. Red stripes mean a bit is left over, so that size doesn't work. Find the biggest size with nothing left over on either ribbon.

Step 3

Which one does the story want?

Before doing any math, decide: is the answer bigger than both numbers, or smaller than both?

LCM

  • "together again", "next time"
  • "the same number of" when buying packs
  • "smallest"

Answer is at least as big as the bigger number.

GCF

  • "greatest", "biggest"
  • "cut into pieces", "split", "identical bags"
  • "nothing left over"

Answer is at most the smaller number.

Step 4 · The fast way for big numbers

Build both numbers from prime blocks

Every number is made by multiplying prime blocks. Blocks that both numbers have go in the middle. GCF = multiply the middle. LCM = multiply every block, using the shared ones only once.
