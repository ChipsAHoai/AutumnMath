Seeing Fractions

A fraction is a bar cut into equal pieces. The bottom number says how many pieces the whole bar is cut into. The top number says how many pieces you have.

For grown-ups: Why adding fractions is tricky
  • It looks like you can add straight across. 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5. Step 3 shows why: 2/5 is smaller than the 1/2 you started with.
  • You can only add pieces of the same size. That means cutting both bars into smaller matching pieces first, which uses the LCM.
  • The app wants the answer simplified. Simplifying is gluing pieces back together, which uses the GCF.
Step 1

Top and bottom

The bottom cuts each bar into equal pieces. The top colors pieces in. If the top is bigger than the bottom, you need more than one whole bar.

Step 2

Same amount, smaller pieces

Cut every piece into smaller pieces. The colored amount doesn't change, but now there are more pieces on top and on the bottom. Multiply both by the same number.

Step 3

Adding: make the pieces match

You can only add pieces that are the same size. Cut both bars until their pieces match, then count all the colored pieces together.

Step 4

Simplify: glue pieces back together

If the pieces can be glued into bigger equal groups, with nothing left over on top or bottom, the fraction gets simpler. The biggest group that works is the GCF of the top and bottom.
